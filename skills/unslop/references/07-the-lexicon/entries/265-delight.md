#### 265. `delight`

- Kind: noun and verb. Banned forms: `delight`, `delights`, `delighted`, `delighting`. The adjective `delightful` has its own entry.
- Why it reads as machine text: `Delight your users` and `moments of delight` come from 2010s product design culture, and models repeat them as a goal for every feature. The word aims at a feeling nobody measures and hides the usual case, in which the user wants the task to finish. It also justifies confetti and bouncy animation over speed.
- Where it shows up: product principles, design critiques, marketing copy.

```slop
Small animations add moments of delight to the experience.
We're obsessed with delighting our customers.
You'll be delighted by how easy setup is.
```

Replace with:

- The task outcome: "the export finishes in 3 seconds".
- "pleased", "glad" or "happy" in personal messages, when the feeling is real.
- The detail users notice: "the form keeps your input after an error".
- Cut it when the sentence promises the reader a feeling.

Rewrites:

```slop
Small animations add moments of delight to the experience.
```

After: The save button confirms in place within 100 ms, with a 120 ms fade and no other animation.

```slop
We're obsessed with delighting our customers.
```

After: We reply to every support email within 4 business hours and call every customer who cancels.

```slop
You'll be delighted by how easy setup is.
```

After: Setup takes 3 steps: connect GitHub, pick a repo, and press deploy.

- Literal use: for the sweet, write "lokum" or put `Turkish delight` in inline code.
