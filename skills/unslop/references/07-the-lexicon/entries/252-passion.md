#### 252. `passion`

- Kind: noun. Banned forms: `passion`, `passions`, and avoid `passionate` for the same reason.
- Why it reads as machine text: `passion` is the default way models express commitment: `our passion for quality`, `passionate about`. Every company and every candidate claims it, so it tells the reader nothing about this team. It replaces evidence of care, such as hours spent or details fixed, with a feeling.
- Where it shows up: about pages, cover letters and bios, job ads.

```slop
Our passion for design drives everything we do.
I have a deep passion for clean code.
Join a team with a passion for innovation.
```

Replace with:

- What the person or team has done that shows care: hours, projects, details.
- "interest" or "focus" when a neutral noun is needed.
- "I like" or "I care about" in personal writing, followed by an example.
- Cut it when the sentence states a feeling and nothing else.

Rewrites:

```slop
Our passion for design drives everything we do.
```

After: We test every screen at 320px wide and with a screen reader before release.

```slop
I have a deep passion for clean code.
```

After: I maintain two open source linters and have reviewed about 600 pull requests on them.

```slop
Join a team with a passion for innovation.
```

After: The team ships to production about 30 times a week and joins a customer call every Friday.

- Literal use: for the fruit, write "maracuja" or put `passion fruit` in inline code; in art history or theology, put `Passion` in inline code.
