#### 248. `bandwidth`

- Kind: noun. Banned forms: `bandwidth`, `bandwidths`.
- Why it reads as machine text: Models use `bandwidth` for a person's time or attention: `I don't have the bandwidth`, `free up bandwidth`. It borrows a network term to make a scheduling problem sound technical. It hides the actual constraint, hours in a week or people on a team, and it makes a refusal vaguer than it needs to be.
- Where it shows up: Slack messages and emails, staffing memos, project plans.

```slop
I don't have the bandwidth to take this on right now.
Hiring a contractor will free up bandwidth for the core team.
Our team has limited bandwidth this quarter.
```

Replace with:

- "time", with hours: "I have 4 free hours this week".
- "capacity" for teams, with headcount or points.
- A plain "no" or "not before", with a date.
- Cut it when the refusal works without a reason.

Rewrites:

```slop
I don't have the bandwidth to take this on right now.
```

After: I can't take this before the 20th; the release needs all my time until then.

```slop
Hiring a contractor will free up bandwidth for the core team.
```

After: The contractor will handle billing support, which gives the 3 core engineers back about 10 hours a week.

```slop
Our team has limited bandwidth this quarter.
```

After: Two of our five engineers are on the migration until March, so we will take on one new feature this quarter.

- Literal use: for network capacity, write "throughput" or give the figure in Mbps or Gbps; in signal processing, give the range in Hz.
