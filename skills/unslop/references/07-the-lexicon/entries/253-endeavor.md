#### 253. `endeavor`

- Kind: noun and verb. Banned forms: `endeavor`, `endeavors`, `endeavored`, `endeavoring`, and the British `endeavour`.
- Why it reads as machine text: `endeavor` is a formal word for "try" or "project" that models use to sound earnest, above all in sign-offs (`best of luck in your future endeavors`). As a noun it inflates ordinary work into a grand attempt and hides what the work is. As a verb (`we endeavor to`) it hedges a promise the writer could make or decline.
- Where it shows up: email closings, mission statements, cover letters.

```slop
Best of luck in all your future endeavors.
We endeavor to respond to every inquiry within 24 hours.
Building a startup is a challenging endeavor.
```

Replace with:

- "try" or "aim" for the verb, and drop it entirely when you can promise.
- "project", "work" or "job" for the noun.
- The next thing, named: "good luck at the clinic".
- Cut it when the sentence is a sign-off; end the email on the last useful line.

Rewrites:

```slop
Best of luck in all your future endeavors.
```

After: Good luck with the new job at the clinic.

```slop
We endeavor to respond to every inquiry within 24 hours.
```

After: We reply to every email within 24 hours on weekdays.

```slop
Building a startup is a challenging endeavor.
```

After: Starting a company means raising money, hiring and selling, often in the same week.

- Literal use: for the Space Shuttle or the ship, write `Endeavour` in inline code.
