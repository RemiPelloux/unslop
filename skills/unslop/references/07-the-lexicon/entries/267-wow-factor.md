#### 267. `wow factor`

- Kind: noun phrase. Banned forms: `wow factor`, `wow-factor`.
- Why it reads as machine text: `wow factor` is a model's way to praise a visual effect or a demo without saying what it does. It shows up in design advice (`add a wow factor to your hero`) that leads to gradients, glows and parallax. It ranks first impression above use and gives no detail anyone can check.
- Where it shows up: design feedback, real estate and event copy, pitch advice.

```slop
Add some wow factor to your landing page with a 3D hero.
The kitchen has serious wow factor.
Our demo needs more wow factor for the investor meeting.
```

Replace with:

- The feature that draws attention: the size, the material, the result shown.
- "first impression" when that is the point, with what the viewer sees in the first 5 seconds.
- For demos, the result the buyer wants, shown with real data.
- Cut it when the advice would add decoration; drop the effect as well.

Rewrites:

```slop
Add some wow factor to your landing page with a 3D hero.
```

After: Put a 5-second screen recording of the product at the top of the landing page.

```slop
The kitchen has serious wow factor.
```

After: The kitchen has a 4-meter marble island and a window over the garden.

```slop
Our demo needs more wow factor for the investor meeting.
```

After: Open the investor demo with a real customer's dashboard and their 30% drop in costs.

- Literal use: the phrase has no literal sense; describe what produces the reaction.
