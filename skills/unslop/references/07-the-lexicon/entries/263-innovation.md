#### 263. `innovation`

- Kind: noun. Banned forms: `innovation`, `innovations`. The adjective `innovative` has its own entry.
- Why it reads as machine text: `innovation` is the most common abstract noun in corporate and model-written copy: `driving innovation`, `a culture of innovation`, `innovation hub`. It claims newness without naming any new thing. Readers have seen it on every about page, so it carries no weight.
- Where it shows up: about pages, job ads, R&D and government announcements.

```slop
We foster a culture of innovation across every team.
The new campus will serve as a hub for innovation.
Innovation is in our DNA.
```

Replace with:

- The new thing, named: product, method or patent, with a date.
- "new", "first" or "change" when a plain word works.
- "research" for R&D, with the budget or headcount.
- Cut it when the sentence contains no new thing.

Rewrites:

```slop
We foster a culture of innovation across every team.
```

After: Every engineer gets one day every two weeks for their own projects; 3 of our current products started that way.

```slop
The new campus will serve as a hub for innovation.
```

After: The new campus houses 400 researchers working on battery chemistry and grid storage.

```slop
Innovation is in our DNA.
```

After: We have filed 22 patents since 2018, most of them on low-power radio.

- Literal use: in economics papers that measure it, write "new products and processes" or put `innovation` in inline code.
