#### 279. `ultimately`

- Kind: adverb, connective. Banned forms: `ultimately`.
- Why it reads as machine text: `ultimately` is the most common closing adverb in model answers, as in `Ultimately, the choice depends on your needs`. It promises a final verdict and then hands the decision back to the reader. It also opens closing paragraphs that restate the body.
- Where it shows up: last paragraphs of comparison answers, opinion pieces, recommendations.

```slop
Ultimately, the best framework depends on your specific needs.
Ultimately, it's up to you to decide.
The goal is ultimately to make users happy.
```

Replace with:

- The verdict, with its condition: "pick Vue if your team knows templates; pick React if you need React Native".
- "in the end" or "finally", only for order in time.
- A tie-breaker rule when the options are close.
- Cut it when the sentence works without it, which it nearly always does; delete sentences that hand the choice back.

Rewrites:

```slop
Ultimately, the best framework depends on your specific needs.
```

After: Use SvelteKit for a small site you build alone; use Next.js if you need its larger hiring pool and plugin count.

```slop
Ultimately, it's up to you to decide.
```

After: If both options pass your tests, pick the one with fewer dependencies.

```slop
The goal is ultimately to make users happy.
```

After: The goal is that a new user finishes setup in under 5 minutes.

- Literal use: for the last step in a sequence, write "finally" or "in the end".
