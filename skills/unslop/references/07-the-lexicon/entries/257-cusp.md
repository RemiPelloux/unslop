#### 257. `cusp`

- Kind: noun. Banned forms: `cusp`, `cusps`. The phrase `on the cusp` has its own entry.
- Why it reads as machine text: Models write `on the cusp of` to announce that something is about to happen, which lets them make a prediction with no date. It is common in trend pieces and launch posts (`on the cusp of a new era`). The word's exact meaning in geometry and dentistry lends the prose a precision it does not have.
- Where it shows up: trend articles, investor updates, launch announcements.

```slop
We are on the cusp of a new era in personal computing.
The company is on the cusp of profitability.
Quantum computing sits on the cusp of mainstream adoption.
```

Replace with:

- "about to" or "close to", with the date or the gap.
- The current number and the threshold: "$40k a month short of break-even".
- "expected in", plus a quarter and a source.
- Cut it when there is no date or number; the claim is a guess.

Rewrites:

```slop
We are on the cusp of a new era in personal computing.
```

After: Three phone makers plan to ship on-device assistants in the second half of 2025.

```slop
The company is on the cusp of profitability.
```

After: The company expects to break even in Q3, when monthly revenue passes $2.1M.

```slop
Quantum computing sits on the cusp of mainstream adoption.
```

After: Several cloud providers rent quantum hardware today; few companies run production work on it.

- Literal use: in geometry or dentistry, put `cusp` in inline code ("the `cusp` of a molar"); in astrology, write "the border between two signs".
