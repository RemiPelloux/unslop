#### 268. `prowess`

- Kind: noun. Banned forms: `prowess`.
- Why it reads as machine text: `prowess` is an old word for skill in battle, and models use it to praise technical or athletic skill in a grand tone: `engineering prowess`, `culinary prowess`. It names a level of skill without showing a single result. It is common in bios and in sentences about a company's strength.
- Where it shows up: bios, press releases, sports and food writing.

```slop
The team's engineering prowess is evident in every release.
Showcase your design prowess with a standout portfolio.
Her culinary prowess earned her a loyal following.
```

Replace with:

- "skill", plus the evidence.
- The result: release count, defect rate, awards.
- The specific craft: "she cures her own ham".
- Cut it when the evidence is already in the next sentence.

Rewrites:

```slop
The team's engineering prowess is evident in every release.
```

After: The team ships every two weeks, and the last 20 releases needed no rollback.

```slop
Showcase your design prowess with a standout portfolio.
```

After: Show 3 projects in your portfolio, each with the problem, your sketches and the result.

```slop
Her culinary prowess earned her a loyal following.
```

After: She bakes 200 loaves a day, and the bakery sells out by 10 am.

- Literal use: in historical writing about combat, write "skill at arms" or put `prowess` in inline code.
