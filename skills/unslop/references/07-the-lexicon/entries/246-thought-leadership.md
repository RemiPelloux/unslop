#### 246. `thought leadership`

- Kind: noun phrase. Banned forms: `thought leadership`.
- Why it reads as machine text: Marketing teams file their blog posts and white papers under `thought leadership content`, and models repeat the label as if it were a genre. It names content by the status it hopes to earn, not by what it says. It hides the format (report, essay, talk) and the topic.
- Where it shows up: marketing plans, agency service pages, content calendars.

```slop
We publish thought leadership on the future of work.
Our thought leadership content drives brand awareness.
Download our latest thought leadership report.
```

Replace with:

- The format: "essay", "report", "research", "talk".
- The topic and the data source.
- "articles by our staff" for a company blog.
- Cut it when the title of the piece already tells the reader what it is.

Rewrites:

```slop
We publish thought leadership on the future of work.
```

After: We publish a quarterly report on remote hiring, based on 8,000 job posts.

```slop
Our thought leadership content drives brand awareness.
```

After: Our engineers write two posts a month about running Postgres at scale; those posts bring in about 30% of trial signups.

```slop
Download our latest thought leadership report.
```

After: Download the 2025 report on checkout abandonment across 400 online stores.

- Literal use: the phrase has no literal sense; name the format and the topic.
