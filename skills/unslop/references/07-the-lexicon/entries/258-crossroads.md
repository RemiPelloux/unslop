#### 258. `crossroads`

- Kind: noun. Banned forms: `crossroads`, and the phrase `at a crossroads`.
- Why it reads as machine text: `at a crossroads` lets a model dramatize a decision without describing the options. It is common in opinion essays and in company updates written before a change. It hides the choice, the trade-offs and the deadline, which are the three things a reader of a decision needs.
- Where it shows up: opinion essays, all-hands notes, industry analysis.

```slop
The web is at a crossroads.
Our company stands at a crossroads.
Open source finds itself at a crossroads between community and commerce.
```

Replace with:

- "has to choose between X and Y", with both options named.
- "decision", plus the date it must be made.
- A table of the options with their costs.
- Cut it when the following paragraph lists the options; start there.

Rewrites:

```slop
The web is at a crossroads.
```

After: Browser makers disagree on third-party cookies; Safari and Firefox block them by default, and Chrome does not.

```slop
Our company stands at a crossroads.
```

After: By June we must decide whether to raise a Series B or cut costs to break even on current revenue.

```slop
Open source finds itself at a crossroads between community and commerce.
```

After: Maintainers of popular projects choose between staying unpaid, charging for hosting, or changing the license, as Redis did in 2024.

- Literal use: for a road junction, write "junction" or "intersection".
