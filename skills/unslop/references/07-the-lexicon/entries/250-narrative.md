#### 250. `narrative`

- Kind: noun, also an adjective. Banned forms: `narrative`, `narratives`.
- Why it reads as machine text: Models use `narrative` for any story, message or explanation, and `control the narrative` or `the narrative around X` for public opinion. It borrows the tone of media studies and turns a set of claims into one shape without stating the claims. In product writing, `our narrative` usually means a message nobody has written down.
- Where it shows up: PR and communications plans, essays on public debate, launch docs.

```slop
We need to own the narrative around the outage.
The deck tells a compelling narrative about our growth.
This challenges the dominant narrative about remote work.
```

Replace with:

- "story" for a sequence of events.
- "explanation", "account" or "message", with the content stated.
- "the common view that", followed by the view.
- Cut it when you can write the claims themselves.

Rewrites:

```slop
We need to own the narrative around the outage.
```

After: Publish the outage timeline and root cause within 24 hours, before others guess at them.

```slop
The deck tells a compelling narrative about our growth.
```

After: The deck shows revenue growing from $1M to $6M in two years and names the 3 deals behind it.

```slop
This challenges the dominant narrative about remote work.
```

After: Most coverage says remote teams ship slower; our data from 200 teams shows no difference in cycle time.

- Literal use: for a form section with that title, such as a grant application or police report, put `Narrative` in inline code or write "written account".
