#### 278. `crucially`

- Kind: adverb, connective. Banned forms: `crucially`, and the adjective `crucial` (its own entry).
- Why it reads as machine text: `crucially` is a louder `importantly`, and models use it to raise the stakes of an ordinary step. It is a form of `crucial`, one of the most overused model adjectives. It tells the reader to worry without saying what fails.
- Where it shows up: tutorials, policy explainers, analysis essays.

```slop
Crucially, the function must be called before render.
Crucially, the agreement includes a non-compete clause.
The team, crucially, had buy-in from leadership.
```

Replace with:

- The failure: "if you call it after render, the first frame shows unstyled text".
- "because", to show why the fact matters.
- The condition placed in a numbered step or a warning.
- Cut it when the fact carries its own weight.

Rewrites:

```slop
Crucially, the function must be called before render.
```

After: Call `init()` before `render()`; otherwise the first frame draws without styles.

```slop
Crucially, the agreement includes a non-compete clause.
```

After: The agreement bars you from working for a competitor for 12 months after you leave.

```slop
The team, crucially, had buy-in from leadership.
```

After: The VP of engineering approved the plan, so the team got 2 extra hires for it.

- Literal use: the word has no literal sense; write "because" and the reason.
