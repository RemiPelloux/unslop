#### Tells

1. A cutout product photo floating on a purple-to-pink gradient with `drop-shadow-2xl`, tilting on hover over 700ms.
2. A "Bestseller" pill with a fire emoji and no ranking behind it.
3. A product name with "Pro X" and a coffee emoji.
4. Five star emoji and a 4.9 from 2,847 reviews that the page never shows.
5. A struck-through "was" price at double the sale price with a red -50% pill: a reference price that, in most catalogs, was never charged.
6. Copy of `elevate`, `premium`, `crafted with`, `meticulous`, `flawless`, and "like never before". Not one dimension or material.
7. A pulsing countdown and "Only 3 left" that reset on reload: a false-urgency pattern regulators treat as a deceptive practice.
8. A violet-to-indigo gradient pill button that grows on hover.
9. A trust row of icons and two-word claims. "Easy Returns" with no period, "`Premium` Quality" with no meaning.

#### Notes

- **The description is numbers.** Burr size, capacity, grind time, weight. A buyer comparing grinders compares these; adjectives do not survive the comparison.
- **No reference price.** EU rules require any announced discount to show the lowest price of the previous 30 days, and the FTC treats an invented "was" price as deceptive. Run a sale when there is one, with real numbers.
- **Stock and urgency are true or absent.** "In stock" plus a ship window answers the actual question, which is when the parcel arrives.
- **The photo sits on `--paper-2` with a 1px rule.** One front view, then burrs, dial and an in-hand shot for scale. Thumbnails are buttons with labels, so they work by keyboard.
- **The first review shown is a 3.** Showing the reason for a lower score, and how support handled it, sells more reliably than a wall of fives.
